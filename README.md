## The entry

`main.py` writes one line — a roast of a smart fridge that locks itself after midnight — to `/output/entry.txt`, and validates it first (1–200 characters, not only whitespace, a single line):

```text
My fridge locks itself at midnight like it has a bedtime — buddy, you're an appliance, not my dad, and those leftovers are mine.
```

## Why this line works

The joke aims straight at the fridge's petty rule, not at smart homes in general. A fridge with a bedtime is a fridge that has given itself a curfew — a rule no one asked a kitchen appliance to enforce, and one that exists only to keep a grown adult away from their own leftovers. "Buddy, you're an appliance, not my dad" is the turn: it demotes the fridge from household authority back to what it is, a box that keeps things cold, and calls out the absurdity of taking orders from it. "And those leftovers are mine" lands the punchline as something a person would actually say out loud at the door at 12:01 a.m. — the owner reclaiming food the fridge has no right to guard. One sentence, one joke, nothing wasted, and the fridge ends the line smaller than it started it.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py
```
